Throughout the academic year I spent at Imperial, I was a writer, copyeditor, and data analyst for *Felix*, the Imperial College London student-run newspaper. In any of these three capacities, I contributed to over 15 issues of the weekly newspaper, whether by plotting visualizations of student body surveys on alcohol/drug use and sexuality; writing timely reviews of books, theatre, and international relations; or by correcting spelling, grammar, and sentence flow errors in the hours leading up to printing. In the spring of 2026, I was also on the editorial team of *Phoenix*, the literary/arts magazine produced by *Felix* (and founded some 80-odd years ago by H.G. Wells), where I was mainly providing feedback on poetry submissions. The [final product](https://phoenixzine.org/static/issues/03.pdf) is very cool (in my completely unbiased opinion.)

Of the myriad hats I wore at *Felix*, my favorite was that of a writer. It was a wonderful return to my liberal arts roots to write about anything, especially in an academic system like the UK's which places so much emphasis on timed exams and so little on long-term reflection. It was especially wonderful to write for a publication that was so keen to promote plurality of self: at its core, *Felix*'s existence acknowledges that intellectual siloing is a folly, and that it is necessary to engage with all types of thinking to truly get to the root of today's most pressing issues. All the pieces I wrote for *Felix* are available at this nifty [link](https://felixonline.co.uk/author/emrys/).
