My name is Emrys (rhymes with bliss). It's lovely to meet you.

I am currently a Predoctoral Scholar in the [Data and Democracy Lab](https://data-democracy.org/) at the University of Chicago, under PI [Moon Duchin](https://computerscience.uchicago.edu/people/moon-duchin/), where I am working on a variety of projects regarding voting systems, social choice theory, gerrymandering, and statistical methods related to those issues. Prior to this, I completed an  MSc Statistics at Imperial College London, where I developed neural surrogate methods for scalable Bayesian inference of geospatial data, supervised by [Oliver Ratmann](https://profiles.imperial.ac.uk/oliver.ratmann05). I was also a [writer](https://felixonline.co.uk/author/emrys/) and copyeditor for *Felix*, the student newspaper at Imperial.

I received my Bachelor's of Arts from [Pomona College](https://www.pomona.edu/about) in Claremont, California, with majors in Mathematics (Statistics) and Spanish. While at Pomona, I wrote my undergraduate thesis in mathematics on the historical confluence of statistics and eugenics circa 1900, supervised by [Jemma Lorenat](https://www.jemmalorenat.com/) and [Shahriar Shahriari](https://www.pomona.edu/directory/people/shahriar-shahriari). I also completed a senior project in Spanish Literature, supervised by [Susana Chávez-Silverman](https://www.pomona.edu/directory/people/susana-chavez-silverman), tracing the coexistence of queer women and oceanic imagery in the short stories of Mayra Santos Febres.

Broadly, I am interested in how humans shape the technology they use, and how technologies shape the humans who use them. I look for perspectives on these themes from both STEM and the social sciences, including but not limited to statistics/probability, history, sociology, and the history and philosophy of science. Furthermore, I find feminist, antiracist, and postcolonial approaches to these topics especially fascinating. If you would like to chat (or swap article/book recommendations), please send me an email!
