My name is Emrys, and I use he/they pronouns. It's lovely to meet you.

I am currently a Predoctoral Scholar in the [Data and Democracy Lab](https://data-democracy.org/) at the University of Chicago, under PI [Moon Duchin](https://computerscience.uchicago.edu/people/moon-duchin/), where I am working on a variety of projects regarding voting systems, social choice theory, gerrymandering, and statistical methods related to those issues.

I completed my MSc in Statistics at Imperial College London. My research there focused on neural surrogate methods for Bayesian inference of gaussian processes, and was supervised by [Oliver Ratmann](https://profiles.imperial.ac.uk/oliver.ratmann05). For more information, see my projects page!

Prior to Imperial, I received my Bachelor's of Arts from [Pomona College](https://www.pomona.edu/about) in Claremont, California, with majors in Mathematics and Spanish. I wrote my undergraduate thesis in mathematics on the historical confluence of statistics and eugenics circa 1900, supervised by [Jemma Lorenat](https://www.jemmalorenat.com/) and [Shahriar Shahriari](https://www.pomona.edu/directory/people/shahriar-shahriari). I also completed a senior project in Spanish Literature, supervised by [Susana Chávez-Silverman](https://www.pomona.edu/directory/people/susana-chavez-silverman), tracing the coexistence of queer women and oceanic imagery in the short stories of Mayra Santos Febres.

Broadly, I am interested in how humans shape the technology they use, and how technologies shape the humans who use them. My favorite perspectives on these themes are interdisciplinary by nature; I believe that quantitative and qualitative analyses are not competitors but *necessarily* collaborators. If you would like to chat about these topics, swap reading recommendations, or simply say hello, please do not hesitate to reach me at: emrys.king25 at imperial dot ac dot uk.
